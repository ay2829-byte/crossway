Growth marketing interview: the metrics questions you'll get

CAC, LTV, payback, ROAS and incrementality: the numbers growth interviews test, with worked examples so you can answer with confidence.

Growth interviews are numbers interviews. You'll be asked to calculate, but more often to explain what a number means and what you'd do about it. Here are the questions that come up most, with worked examples.

CAC and payback

You spent $30,000 and got 1,500 customers. What's your CAC? $20. Then the follow up: is that good? It depends on what a customer is worth.

Customers pay $50 a month at 80% gross margin. How long until you earn back a $300 CAC? Monthly gross profit is $40, so payback is 300 ÷ 40 = 7.5 months.

LTV

A $20 a month plan with 5% monthly churn. What's lifetime revenue per customer? Roughly 20 ÷ 0.05 = $400. Mention that you'd use gross profit, not revenue, to compare against CAC, and that churn often isn't constant.

ROAS isn't profit

$20,000 in ads drove $70,000 in sales. Good? ROAS is 3.5x, but at a 40% margin that's $28,000 of gross profit, so $8,000 after ad spend. Always bring it back to profit.

Incrementality

The ad platform reports 4,000 conversions. A holdout test shows 1,500 more conversions in regions with ads than without. Which number do you trust? The holdout. Only 1,500 conversions were caused by ads. At $60,000 spend, the true cost per conversion is $40, not the $15 the platform implies.

Funnels and virality

Each user invites 4 people and 20% accept. What happens? The viral coefficient is 0.8. Each wave is smaller than the last, so 1,000 seed users grow to about 5,000 total before invites run out.

How to answer well

  • Say the formula out loud before calculating
  • Sanity check the result: does it make sense for this business?
  • End with a decision: what would you do next?

Questions

What metrics do growth marketing interviews ask about?

CAC, LTV, payback period, ROAS, conversion rates through a funnel, churn and incrementality.

How do you calculate CAC payback?

Divide CAC by monthly gross profit per customer. A $300 CAC with $40 a month of gross profit pays back in 7.5 months.

Why does incrementality matter?

Ad platforms count conversions that would have happened anyway. Holdout tests show what ads actually caused.
