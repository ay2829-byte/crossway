How many jobs should you apply to per week?

More applications rarely mean more interviews. Here's how to pick a weekly number you can do well, and the signals that tell you to change course.

There's no magic number. Someone sending 200 applications a week and someone sending 5 can both be doing it wrong. What matters is how many good applications you can send, and whether they're working.

Why volume stops working

Bulk applying feels productive. But generic applications land in the same pile as everyone else's, often filtered by keywords before a person sees them. If every application takes two minutes, it probably looks like it took two minutes.

A weekly number you can do well

A sustainable starting point for most people looking full time:

  • 5 to 10 targeted applications to roles that genuinely fit, each with a tailored first line and one piece of proof.
  • 3 to 5 direct messages to hiring managers or people on the team.
  • 2 to 3 hours of practice on the skills those roles ask for.

If you're working while you look, halve it. Consistency beats bursts.

Track one number: your response rate

Keep a simple list: role, date, and whether you heard back. After about 20 applications you'll see your response rate.

  • Under 5%: your targeting or your materials are off. Apply to fewer, closer fits, and lead with proof.
  • 5 to 15%: you're in a normal range. Keep going and improve the weakest step.
  • Over 15%: you're doing well. If interviews aren't turning into offers, shift time to interview practice.

What a good application has

  • A first line that shows you read the posting
  • One concrete result, with a number if you have it
  • A link to something they can check: a case study, a project, a solved problem

Protect your energy

Job hunting is a long game. Pick a weekly number you can keep up for two months, not one you can manage for a week. Block time on your calendar, stop at your number, and spend the rest of the week getting better at the work itself.

Questions

Is applying to 100 jobs a week too many?

For most people, yes. At that pace applications get generic. Fewer, targeted applications with proof usually get more replies.

What is a good response rate for job applications?

It varies by field and level, but if fewer than 1 in 20 applications get a reply, rethink your targeting and materials.

Should I keep applying while interviewing?

Yes. Keep a smaller steady pace until you have an offer you've accepted.
