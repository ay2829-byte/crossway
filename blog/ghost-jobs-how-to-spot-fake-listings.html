Ghost jobs: how to spot fake listings before you apply

Some job posts are never going to be filled. Here are the signs of a ghost job, how to check a listing in two minutes, and where to spend your time instead.

You find a role that fits, spend an hour on the application, and hear nothing. Sometimes that's a busy recruiter. Sometimes the job was never really open. These are ghost jobs: listings that stay up with no real plan to hire for them soon.

Why ghost jobs exist

Companies leave roles up for reasons that have nothing to do with you. A team wants a pipeline of candidates "just in case". A budget got frozen but nobody took the post down. A role is already promised to an internal person, but the policy says it has to be posted. Or the listing was copied to dozens of job sites and the copies never got cleaned up.

Seven signs a listing might be a ghost

  • It's been up for months. A role that's been open for 90 days or more, or keeps getting reposted with a fresh date, is worth a second look.
  • You can't find it on the company's own careers page. If it only lives on job boards, the original may already be closed.
  • It's vague. No team, no manager, no real responsibilities, just a list of buzzwords.
  • There's no pay range in a state that requires one. Colorado, California, New York and Washington all have pay transparency laws for most postings.
  • It says "talent community" or "future opportunities". That's a pipeline, not a job.
  • The company just announced layoffs or a hiring freeze and the role is still up.
  • Hundreds of applicants and no movement. If the applicant count keeps climbing for weeks and nobody you know has heard back, be careful.

The two minute check

Before you apply to anything, do this:

  • Open the company's careers page and search for the exact title. If it's there and dated recently, good sign.
  • Search the company name plus "layoffs" or "hiring freeze" for the last three months.
  • Look up the hiring team. If you can find the manager, a short note to them beats a cold application.

Go to the source

The simplest way to avoid ghost jobs is to look where they're least likely to hide: the company's own applicant tracking system. When a role closes there, it disappears. Copies on big job boards can linger for weeks.

That's how Crossway's job board works. Every few hours it reads open roles directly from company career pages on Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday and others. When a role closes at the source, it's gone from Crossway on the next refresh. Roles older than four months are dropped automatically.

Spend your time where it counts

Ghost jobs hurt most when you apply in bulk. Ten careful applications to roles that are clearly live will beat a hundred to listings that might not be. Pair each one with proof: a short case study or a few real work problems you've solved, so the right person has something to look at.

Questions

What is a ghost job?

A job listing that stays posted with no real plan to fill it soon. It might be a pipeline post, a frozen role, or a stale copy on a job board.

How can I tell if a job posting is real?

Check that it's on the company's own careers page with a recent date, that it names a team and real responsibilities, and that the company isn't in a hiring freeze.

Are ghost jobs illegal?

Generally no, though some states now require pay ranges on postings. The practical fix is to apply where listings come straight from company career pages.
