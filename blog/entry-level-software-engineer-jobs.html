Entry level software engineer jobs right now: 1,441 open roles, who's hiring, and what they pay

We counted 3,981 US software engineer roles. 1,441 are entry level, defense and aerospace lead hiring, and typical entry pay is $125k to $162k. What that means for your search.

New grad software engineering has been a tough market for a few years, and the headlines make it sound closed. The data says something more nuanced. In early November 2026, Crossway's feed held 3,981 software engineer roles in the US posted in the previous 45 days, with 2,077 posted in the last week. Of those, 1,441 are entry level by title or by accepting 0 to 2 years of experience. The openings are there. They're just concentrated in places many new grads aren't looking.

Aerospace and defense dominate. Across all SWE levels, the leaders are SpaceX (310), Anduril (291), JPMorgan Chase (184), Apple (143), Roblox (117) and NVIDIA (108). Two practical points:

  • Many defense and aerospace roles require US citizenship or a clearance. If you're an international student, filter those out early. If you're a US citizen, these employers are one of the biggest openings in the market.
  • Banks and fintech hire steadily. JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Capital One run large early career programs and are less exposed to tech hiring swings.

What entry level engineers earn

Among entry level SWE postings that list pay, the typical range is $125k to $162k. Across all SWE levels it's $167k to $235k, and fully remote SWE roles list a typical $190k to $255k, because most remote roles are senior. 1,711 of the 3,981 postings list pay, thanks largely to pay transparency laws in California, New York, Washington and Colorado.

What they ask for

The most requested skills across SWE postings are Python, C++, APIs, Java, system design, Go, AWS and Kubernetes. The strong showing for C++ reflects aerospace, defense, robotics and trading. If you're aiming at those employers, C++ and systems fundamentals matter more than the latest web framework. For product companies, Python, Java or Go plus API design and cloud basics cover most postings.

How interviews are changing

Algorithm questions haven't gone away, but more teams now add practical rounds: debug a failing service, review a pull request, extend a small codebase or design a simple system. Companies have also gotten more careful about AI assisted cheating in online assessments, so expect more live rounds and follow up questions about your own code.

  • Keep practicing core data structures and algorithms. See is LeetCode still worth it for how much.
  • Add real work practice: reading unfamiliar code, finding bugs, writing tests. Crossway Arena has real bug fixing problems with replayable solutions.
  • Learn enough system design to talk through a simple service, including back of the envelope estimates.

A search strategy that fits the market

  1. Widen your target list beyond consumer tech: aerospace, defense (if eligible), banks, healthcare, logistics and industrial companies all hire engineers.
  2. Apply early in the week a role is posted. Over half the SWE roles in our feed were posted in the last 7 days.
  3. Prioritize roles that accept 0 to 2 years and entry level titles.
  4. Show proof: one or two projects with real users or real complexity, a clean GitHub and solved problems you can explain.
  5. Use referrals wherever you can. Early career programs get huge applicant pools.

Where the roles are

The San Francisco Bay Area leads with over 1,200 SWE roles, followed by New York, Seattle, Los Angeles and Boston. Entry level roles at aerospace and defense employers are often outside the usual tech hubs, in places like Southern California, Texas, Florida, Colorado and Washington. Projects that help new grad engineers stand out

  • Something people use: a tool for your club, a browser extension, a bot for a community. Real users create real problems to solve and real stories to tell.
  • Something hard: a toy database, a compiler, an emulator, a distributed key value store. These show depth and give great interview material.
  • Something close to the employer: robotics or embedded work for aerospace, a trading simulator for fintech, a data pipeline for data heavy companies.
  • Contributions to open source: even small merged fixes show you can read and work in someone else's codebase.

Two strong projects beat ten tutorials. For each, be ready to explain a design choice you'd change and a bug that took you days.

Resume tips for new grad engineers

  • Put skills and languages near the top, matched to the roles you target.
  • For internships and projects, describe what you built, the scale, and the result: "Cut API latency from 800ms to 120ms by adding a cache for the pricing service."
  • Link GitHub and a short demo video for your best project.
  • Keep it to one page, single column.

Online assessments and live rounds

Many early career pipelines start with an online assessment: two or three coding problems in 60 to 90 minutes. Practice under time limits, and read problems carefully before coding. Live rounds increasingly include follow up questions about your approach, partly because of AI assisted cheating, so be ready to explain complexity, edge cases and alternatives for anything you write.

If you're eligible for clearance roles

US citizens should take defense and aerospace openings seriously. They're the largest source of entry level engineering roles right now, they invest in training, and a security clearance becomes a durable career asset. Expect a longer hiring process, background checks and, for some roles, on site work.

When to apply

Large early career programs often open in late summer and fall for the following year, while smaller companies and defense contractors hire year round. Apply in the first week a role is posted when you can: over half the SWE roles in our feed were posted in the last seven days.

Engineering jobs outside the tech industry

Some of the steadiest early career engineering hiring happens at companies most new grads don't think of as tech companies: banks and payment networks, insurers, retailers with big ecommerce operations, airlines, logistics companies, hospitals and health systems, energy companies, and automakers building software for vehicles. These employers often run structured new grad programs, offer solid pay, and are less exposed to startup funding cycles. Your title is still software engineer, and the experience transfers.

Your first 90 days on the job

  • Ship something small in your first two weeks. A bug fix or a tiny feature teaches you the build, review and deploy process.
  • Write things down. A personal doc of how systems connect, who owns what and what confused you becomes valuable fast.
  • Ask for code reviews early and often. They're the fastest way to learn the team's standards.
  • Understand the business. Know what your team's work does for users and revenue. It shapes better decisions and better promotion cases.

How we counted

US roles posted in the 45 days before early November 2026, pulled from company career pages. A role counts as software engineering if its title matches software engineer or developer, full stack, backend or frontend engineer, excluding test, QA and management roles. Entry level means an entry level or junior title, or a posting that accepts 0 to 2 years. Live numbers are on our software engineer jobs page.

Questions

Are there entry level software engineer jobs right now?

Python, C++, Java, APIs, system design basics, Go, AWS and Kubernetes. C++ is common at aerospace, defense and trading firms.
