Is LinkedIn Premium worth it in 2026?

What LinkedIn Premium actually gives job seekers, when it's worth paying for, when it isn't, and free ways to get the same results.

LinkedIn Premium promises to help you get hired faster. For some people it does. For many, the free version plus a better strategy works just as well. Here's how to decide.

What Premium gives job seekers

  • InMail credits to message people you're not connected with
  • Who viewed your profile, going back further than the free version
  • Applicant insights showing how you compare to other applicants
  • AI writing help for messages and your profile
  • LinkedIn Learning courses, on most Career plans

Plans and prices change often and vary by country, so check the current price before you start a trial. There's usually a free trial for first time users.

When it's worth it

  • You're actively reaching out to hiring managers and need InMail to do it
  • You're in a field where recruiters live on LinkedIn, like enterprise sales
  • You'll use it hard for one or two months, then cancel

When it's not

  • You mostly apply through Easy Apply. Premium doesn't fix the volume problem.
  • You're early in your career with little proof to show yet. Build proof first.
  • You'll forget to cancel. Set a reminder the day you start the trial.

Free ways to get the same results

  • Instead of InMail: find the hiring manager's work email pattern, or connect with a short note that references their work.
  • Instead of applicant insights: track your own response rate. See how many jobs to apply to per week.
  • Instead of profile views: put your proof where companies are looking for people, not just where they post jobs.

Questions

Does LinkedIn Premium help you get a job?

It can help if you use InMail for targeted outreach. It doesn't fix low response rates from mass applying.

Can I cancel LinkedIn Premium anytime?

Yes, through your account settings. Set a reminder before a free trial ends.

What's a free alternative to LinkedIn Premium?

Targeted outreach with short personal notes, tracking your own response rate, and platforms like Crossway where companies invite you based on your work.
