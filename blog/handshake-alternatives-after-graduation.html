Handshake alternatives for after you graduate

Handshake is built for students. Here's where new grads and early career people find roles once they're out of school, and how to stand out without much experience.

Handshake is great while you're in school: campus recruiting, career fairs and employers who want students. After graduation, access and relevance often fade, and many schools treat alumni access differently. Here's where to look next.

Where to look after graduation

  • Crossway. Free. 180,000 roles from company career pages, filtered by level so you can focus on entry and new grad roles, with 10 matches every morning and companies inviting you based on your work. It also includes open new grad and internship lists from Simplify.
  • Company career pages. Many new grad programs open once a year. Note the dates for companies you like.
  • Wellfound. Startups that often care more about skills than experience.
  • LinkedIn. Still useful for alumni connections and recruiter outreach.
  • Your alumni network. A message to someone from your school at a company you like is one of the warmest leads there is.

Stand out without much experience

The hardest part of the first job is the experience you don't have yet. Proof closes the gap.

  • Solve real work problems in your field and keep a record. A timed product case or SQL problem says more than a GPA.
  • Write one case study from a class project or internship: the problem, what you did, the result.
  • Do a daily drill. Ten minutes a day builds a record companies can actually see.

Don't wait for new grad season

Structured programs hire on fixed calendars, but smaller companies hire all year. Apply to both, keep a steady weekly pace, and see the best job sites for entry level jobs for more options.

Questions

Can I use Handshake after I graduate?

Many schools keep alumni access for some time, but it varies. Check with your career center.

Where do new grads find jobs?

Company career pages, Crossway, Wellfound, LinkedIn and alumni networks are the main places.

How do I get a job with no experience?

Build proof: solved problems in your field, a case study from a project, and a steady record of practice that companies can see.
