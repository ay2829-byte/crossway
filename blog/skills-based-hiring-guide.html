Skills based hiring: a practical guide for small teams

How small teams can hire on proven skills instead of degrees and job titles: defining the skills, testing them fairly, and running a lightweight process.

Skills based hiring means judging people on what they can do, not where they studied or which titles they've held. For small teams it's often the best way to find great people that big companies overlook.

Why it works for small teams

  • You compete for talent without competing on brand name.
  • Career changers and self taught people bring skills that resumes hide.
  • A clear skills test makes hiring decisions faster and fairer.

Step 1: define the skills, not the resume

Write down three to five things the person must be able to do in their first 90 days. Not "5 years of experience" but "can write SQL to answer product questions" or "can run and read an A/B test". Drop requirements that don't map to real work, like a specific degree.

Step 2: test them fairly

  • Use short, realistic problems from your own work.
  • Give everyone the same problem and the same time.
  • Score against a simple rubric you wrote before seeing any answers.
  • Hide names, photos and schools while you score, so the work speaks first.

Step 3: keep the process light

  1. A short intro call to check motivation and logistics
  2. One timed skills problem, 30 to 60 minutes
  3. A conversation about their solution and how they'd extend it
  4. A final chat with the team

Four steps, done in a week or two. Long processes lose the best candidates to faster companies.

Common mistakes

  • Tests that are really free work. Keep them small and clearly hypothetical.
  • Puzzles that don't match the job. A data role should test data, not riddles.
  • Skipping the debrief. The conversation after the test is where you learn the most.

Questions

What is skills based hiring?

Hiring people based on demonstrated ability to do the work, rather than degrees, past titles or years of experience.

How do you assess skills fairly?

Use the same realistic problem for everyone, score with a rubric written in advance, and hide names and schools while scoring.

Is skills based hiring slower?

It doesn't have to be. A short timed problem plus a debrief can replace several rounds of resume screening and interviews.
