Laid off? A 30 day plan to land your next role

A calm, week by week plan for the first month after a layoff: what to sort out first, how to tell your story, and how to get back in front of companies fast.

Getting laid off is a shock, even when you saw it coming. The first month sets the tone for the whole search. Here's a plan that puts the urgent things first and keeps you moving without burning out.

Week 1: sort out the basics

  • Read your severance agreement carefully before signing. Note the deadline. It's often reasonable to ask questions or negotiate.
  • File for unemployment in your state right away. Benefits usually start from when you file, not when you lost the job.
  • Sort out health insurance. In the US you typically have 60 days to elect COBRA, and losing coverage also opens a special enrollment window on the marketplace.
  • Get your work samples and references lined up while contacts are fresh. Ask two or three colleagues now.

Week 2: tell your story

Write two sentences you can say without flinching: what happened and what you want next. Layoffs are common, and nobody needs a long explanation. "My team was cut in a company wide restructuring. I'm looking for a product role where I can own growth experiments."

Then update your profile around results, not titles. Three bullets with numbers beat ten without.

Week 3: targeted outreach

  • List 20 companies you'd genuinely like to work for.
  • Message former managers and colleagues. Warm intros are the fastest path back in.
  • Apply to roles that clearly fit, at a pace you can keep. See how many jobs to apply to per week.

Week 4: get interview sharp

Interviews come in bursts. Practice before you need it. Pick the format your field uses, whether that's data analysis questions, product cases or system design, and do a few timed problems each day.

Look after yourself

Keep a routine. Set a daily stopping point. Tell people you trust what's going on. A search usually takes longer than you want, and steady effort beats panic every time.

Questions

How soon should I start applying after a layoff?

Start within the first week or two, once severance, unemployment and insurance are sorted. Early outreach to your network matters most.

How do I explain a layoff in an interview?

Keep it short and factual, then talk about what you want next. Layoffs are common and rarely held against candidates.

Should I take the first offer I get?

Not automatically. Compare it against your runway and goals, and it's fine to ask for a few days to decide.
